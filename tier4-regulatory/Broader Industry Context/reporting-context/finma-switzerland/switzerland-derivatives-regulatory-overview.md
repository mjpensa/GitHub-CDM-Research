# Switzerland Derivatives Regulatory Overview

**Date:** December 2025
**Jurisdiction:** Switzerland
**Regulator:** FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority)

---

## Regulatory Framework

### Financial Market Infrastructure Act (FinMIA)

The Swiss regulatory requirements of the FinMIA provide for reporting obligations regarding OTC derivatives and exchange-traded derivatives (ETDs).

### Trade Repository Reporting

Pursuant to article 104 FMIA, subject to certain limited exceptions, Swiss counterparties are required to report their derivatives trades to a trade repository which has been authorized or recognized by FINMA.

**Reporting Options:**
1. **Submitter (Direct Reporting)** - Trade party reports their trades directly
2. **Non-Submitter (Delegated Reporting)** - Third party reports on behalf of trade party

**Reporting Type:** Single-sided reporting is mandatory for Switzerland (FINMA) jurisdiction.

---

## FinMIA Reform Package (2024-2025)

### Federal Council Proposal (June 19, 2024)

The Swiss Federal Council published a proposed revision of the Financial Market Infrastructure Act containing:
- Amendments to Swiss market infrastructure
- Amendments to Swiss market conduct framework
- Amendments relating to derivatives trading rules
- Changes to reporting obligations
- Counterparty categorization updates
- Cross-border transaction rules

**Consultation Period:** Ended October 11, 2024

**Next Steps:** Federal Council will prepare revised version for Swiss Parliament submission, likely in 2025.

### Key Proposed Changes

**Counterparty Classification:**
The determination of the status of a counterparty as a small or large financial or non-financial counterparty will become an annual determination under the revised rules.

**Small Non-Financial Counterparty Exemption:**
Currently, derivatives transactions must generally be reported to a trade repository, but this is only applicable to small non-financial counterparties where its non-Swiss counterparty does not itself report the derivatives transaction. FINMA has not yet implemented reporting obligations for small non-financial counterparties, and with the adoption of the draft bill, such obligation would be abolished.

## Exemptions

### FX Derivatives

Exemptions from the derivatives regulation apply in respect of:
- Physically settled FX swaps (in addition to physically settled FX forwards)
- Exempted FX swaps are only subject to reporting obligation, but not other regulatory requirements

### Commodity Derivatives

Physically settled OTC derivatives (including swaps) with commodities as underlyings are exempted and not subject to the derivatives regulation under the FinMIA at all.

---

## Substituted Compliance

To the extent that transactions are entered into on a cross-border basis, regulatory requirements may be complied with by applying the rules of a jurisdiction recognized by FINMA as equivalent to the rules of the FinMIA.

**Currently Recognized Equivalent Regimes:**
- EMIR (EU)
- UK EMIR
- US rules of the CFTC

**Important Limitation:**
It is currently not possible to satisfy the reporting obligation under foreign rules that have been recognised as equivalent by FINMA (e.g., EMIR), because there are no foreign trade repositories that are recognized by FINMA for purposes of reporting under a jurisdiction other than the FinMIA.

### FINMA Communication 04/2025 (October 9, 2025)

"Extension of the transitional period applicable to the exchange of collateral in certain OTC derivatives transactions" - further extends the deadline for implementing the exchange of collateral.
