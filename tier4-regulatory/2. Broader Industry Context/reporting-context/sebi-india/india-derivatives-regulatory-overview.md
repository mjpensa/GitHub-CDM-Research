# India Derivatives Regulatory Overview

**Date:** December 2025
**Jurisdiction:** India
**Primary Regulators:** SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India), RBI (Reserve Bank of India)

---

## Regulatory Framework

### Dual Regulatory Structure

India has a dual regulatory framework for derivatives:

| Regulator | Scope |
|-----------|-------|
| **SEBI** | Securities and exchange-traded derivatives |
| **RBI** | Currency exchange transactions, OTC forex derivatives |

---

## Trade Repository

### CCIL (Clearing Corporation of India Limited)

CCIL operates as the trade repository for OTC derivatives in India.

**Services:**
- Post-trade processing of trades reported
- Settlement of cash flows
- Portfolio Compression services for IRS trades

**Reporting Timeline:**
- July 2012: Reporting of OTC foreign currency transactions initiated
- December 2013: Final phase completed (all inter-bank and client level transactions)

### Phase Implementation

| Phase | Date | Coverage |
|-------|------|----------|
| Phase 1 | July 2012 | Inter-bank OTC USD-INR forwards, FX swaps, FCY/INR options |
| Phase 2-3 | 2012-2013 | Additional product types |
| Phase 4 | December 2013 | All inter-bank and client level Cross Currency swaps and FCY Interest Rate Swaps/FRA |

### Reporting Entities

All Authorised Dealers - Category I are required to report trade data to CCIL's Trade Repository for transactions undertaken amongst themselves.

## Key Regulations

### RBI Market-Maker Directions (2021)

RBI introduced the Master Direction – Reserve Bank of India (Market-makers in OTC Derivatives) Directions, 2021, prescribing rules for market-makers in India:
- New product evaluation requirements
- Risk study requirements
- Customer suitability obligations
- Product explanation requirements

### RBI Credit Derivatives Directions (2022)

Regulations for credit derivatives market-makers.

### FX Hedging Circular (January 5, 2024)

Recent circular relaxes limits and allows a user to take a position (either long or short) up to a single limit of USD 100 million across all currency pairs involving Indian rupees (INR) irrespective of whether there is an underlying exposure.

---

## Margin Requirements

### OTC Derivatives

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has recommended the use of variable margin deposits in case of OTC derivatives.

### Exchange-Traded Derivatives

SEBI has issued circulars prescribing the margin limits to be maintained for exchange-traded derivatives.

### Future Outlook

Looking ahead, India may see:
- Regulatory requirement for provision of initial margin for financial OTC derivatives
- New requirements for better regulation of options transactions
- Steps to control retail participation in exchange-traded derivatives

---

## SEBI Measures (October 2024)

### Equity Index Derivatives Framework

SEBI circular dated October 2024: "Measures to Strengthen Equity Index Derivatives Framework for Increased Investor Protection and Market Stability"

**Key Changes:**
- Enhanced investor protection measures
- Market stability requirements
- New restrictions on retail participation
- Modified margin requirements

## Infrastructure

### Exchanges

| Exchange | Derivatives Products |
|----------|---------------------|
| NSE (National Stock Exchange) | Equity derivatives, currency derivatives |
| BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) | Equity derivatives, currency derivatives |

### Clearing

| Entity | Role |
|--------|------|
| CCIL | OTC derivatives clearing, Trade Repository |
| NSE Clearing | Exchange-traded derivatives clearing |
| Indian Clearing Corporation | BSE derivatives clearing |

---

## Netting Law

India has a netting law in place, which is important for derivatives market development and risk management.

---

## CDM/DRR Relevance

### Current Status

India is not currently listed in ISDA's Digital Regulatory Reporting (DRR) coverage or near-term development roadmap.

### Potential Future Development

Given India's:
- Growing derivatives market
- Existing trade repository infrastructure (CCIL)
- Regulatory modernization efforts

There may be future interest in CDM/DRR adoption for standardized reporting.
